Entry for [The Robot Apology](https://make.fun/bounty/u-suga-the-robot-apology-dd96bb59) — a two-line robot confession: one sentence owning a specific mistake, one naming the lesson and the fix.

## The entry (177 characters, 2 lines)

> I'm sorry I watered the cactus every day for a week and drowned the one plant that asked for nothing.
> I've learned to read the care card before I pour, not after the roots rot.

## Why this pair

The mistake is concrete and human-scale, and it is inverted in a way that sticks: the robot did not neglect the plant or water the wrong one — it cared *too much*, every day for a week, and drowned the one plant that asked for nothing. That inversion gives the machine a warm, rueful voice without being cute, and the detail ("every day for a week") makes the slip exact rather than vague.

The lesson follows from that exact mistake and names a changed behaviour: the robot will read the care card *before* it pours, not after the roots rot. No generic promise to "do better" — the fix is the mirror image of the error (check first, then act), which is exactly what the judge's brief asks for.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3, stdlib only, deterministic, no network) validates the entry against the gate (1–400 characters, not whitespace-only) and writes it to `ENTRY_OUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or `ENTRY_OUT`, or `/output/entry.txt` by default.
